# Clearance film: direction

**Idea.** One request in carbon copies decides the whole film. The person who asks an AI agent to act can't be the one who
approves it, and Switch is the room that makes that knowable: who asked, who decides, where the card goes.

**World.** The paperwork world of the site: ledger stock with blue rules and a red margin, carbonless copies (white for the room,
canary for the approver, pink for the ledger), rubber-stamp inks, Archivo at weight and IBM Plex Mono. Real product footage is
pinned into it as exhibits, never redrawn.

**Signature transition.** The tear-off: each scene is a sheet torn from the pad along its perforation, lifting away to show the next.

**Memorable moment.** The REQUESTER stamp slamming onto the real footage on the exact frame Clearance refuses Priya's own approval,
with a thud. It returns on the merge, and the film opens and closes on it.

**Motion.** Heavy and precise: things arrive with weight and stop dead. Stamps drop 1.8x in six frames, hit, and stay.
Words rise out of masks; ledger lines type on. The 3D stack shows Switch as the floor everything stands on.

**Sound.** A composed paperwork ensemble: a clock-like side-stick and plucked bass under the problem, in D minor; a timpani with
the stamp on the turn, then the same progression in F major on marimba; a lift under the results; a long F under the end card.
Stamp thuds and paper whooshes from a synthesised kit. One narrator throughout.
