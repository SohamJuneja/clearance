# Clearance: taste profile

## Personality
IS
- **Exacting.** Hard edges, tight alignment to a form grid, numbers in monospace, nothing decorative that doesn't carry meaning.
- **Accountable.** It shows its working: real screenshots, real hashes, caveats next to every number. Nothing hidden behind hover.
- **Wry.** A dry, human wit, like a stamp that says REQUESTER on a refused approval. Confident, never shouty.

NEVER
- **Corporate SaaS.** No gradient blobs, no glassy cards in a 3-up grid, no stock "trusted by" logos, no vague promises.
- **Hacker cliché.** No green-on-black matrix rain, no neon cyberpunk, no hooded figures, no padlock icons.
- **Fear-selling.** No red alarm aesthetics or "AI is coming for your money". The tone is calm control, not panic.

## Audience taste
Engineers who use Linear, GitHub, Stripe docs, terminal tools and Mattermost. They trust specifics: file paths, commands, exact
numbers with sample sizes. Indian context matters here (₹ amounts, UPI IDs, IST times), shown plainly, not as decoration.

## Brand anchors
- Name "Clearance": keep. Wordmark set in our display face, no logo mark needed; the stamp can be the mark.
- Product UI (Mattermost cards, the Desk): keep as real screenshots, framed like documents, never redrawn as fake UI.
- Copy voice: plain, short sentences, from the user's side (README voice). Keep.
- Colours: none established. New, from the subject: carbonless form colours and stamp inks.

## Feel in motion
Heavy and precise. Things arrive with weight and stop dead, no bounce, no float. A stamp: 140 ms drop, 1 frame squash, ink settles
over 300 ms. Forms slide 500-700 ms on a strong ease-out. Ledger lines type on at about 30 characters per 100 ms. Scroll story is
scrubbed and pinned only where it tells the workflow; everything else reveals once.

## Material and texture
Paper (slight grain, carbonless tints), stamp ink (slightly uneven, multiplied over paper), typewriter and terminal monospace, the
chat UI as a real artefact pinned to the page.

## Taste anchors
- Carbonless triplicate forms: the white/canary/pink stack is instantly "paperwork", and each copy has its own reader.
- Swiss railway and air-traffic strips: dense, ruled, exact, readable at a glance.
- Rubber stamps (APPROVED / DENIED / RECEIVED): authority in one gesture, and slightly imperfect ink.
- Stripe Press and Teenage Engineering product pages: confidence through restraint and craft, and a sense of humour in details.

## Lines not to cross
- No invented UI pretending to be the product. Real screenshots only, labelled.
- No number that isn't in README/RESULTS, and no number without its caveat nearby.
- No more than one signature effect per screen. Content readable with motion off.
- No cream + serif + terracotta, no purple-blue gradients, no near-black + acid green.
